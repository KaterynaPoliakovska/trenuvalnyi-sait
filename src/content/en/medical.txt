## Who translates your medical documents

### Qualified translators with a medical background
Medical documents are translated by specialists who know anatomical, clinical and pharmacological terminology. For notarisation, the translation is made by a qualified translator whose signature is certified by a notary.

### Multi-step proofreading and checking of Latin terms
Every translation is checked by an editor who compares diagnoses, Latin terms, abbreviations, dosages and values with the original. A mistake in a single value can affect treatment, so proofreading is mandatory.

### Full confidentiality and patient data protection (NDA)
Medical data is sensitive information. Only the translator and the manager have access to the documents, and we sign a non-disclosure agreement if needed.

## Certifying medical translations

### Certified with the bureau's seal
For most clinics abroad, a translation certified with the bureau's seal is enough. It is a fast and affordable option.

### Notarisation and apostille
A notarised translation of medical documents in Kyiv is needed for insurance companies, courts and state institutions. An apostille cannot always be placed on medical documents: it depends on who issued the document. That is why the manager checks this in advance. → Apostille

## Frequently asked questions

### Do you translate handwritten documents and certificates with illegible handwriting?
Yes. The translator reads the handwritten text, and words that cannot be read are marked in the translation as [illegible] so as not to distort the meaning. For important documents, we recommend asking the clinic for a printed version.

### Is notarisation needed to submit documents to a foreign clinic?
Usually not: a translation with the bureau's seal is enough for the clinic. Notarisation is needed for insurance companies, courts and state institutions, so it is worth checking the requirements in advance.

### Why does medical translation need extra time for proofreading?
Medical documents contain Latin terms, abbreviations and exact values, and a mistake in one of them can affect a diagnosis or treatment. That is why every translation goes through a separate terminology check.
