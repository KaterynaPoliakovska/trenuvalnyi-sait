## Certifying the translation

### Notarised translation
A notarised birth certificate translation is needed for embassies, consulates and Ukrainian institutions. The notary certifies the translator's signature. → Notarised translation

### Sworn translation for Poland
A sworn translation of a birth certificate is accepted by voivodeship offices, civil registry offices (USC) and schools in Poland without additional notarisation. → Sworn translation

### With apostille
A birth certificate translation with apostille is needed for countries party to the Hague Convention. The apostille is placed on the original certificate, and then the document is translated together with the apostille. → Apostille

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a birth certificate need an apostille?
An apostille is needed if the certificate is submitted to a country party to the Hague Convention and the institution requires it. The apostille on a birth certificate is placed by the Ministry of Justice of Ukraine, before translation. For some countries, including Poland, an apostille on civil registry documents is often not required, but requirements depend on the institution, so it is worth checking in advance.

### How are the parents' details translated?
We write the parents' names and surnames as they appear in their international passports. If there is no passport, we use the official transliteration. That is why we recommend sending copies of international passports together with the certificate, so that names are spelled the same in all documents.
