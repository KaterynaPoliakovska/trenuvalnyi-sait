## Types of interpreting

### Consecutive interpreting
The speaker pauses, and the interpreter conveys what was said after each segment. The best format for negotiations, presentations and interviews.

### Simultaneous interpreting
The interpretation runs at the same time as the speech: the interpreter works from a booth and participants listen through headphones. Suitable for large events but requires equipment and two interpreters.

### Interpreting at conferences and events
A conference interpreter works at forums, seminars and press conferences, studying the programme and presentations in advance to convey specialised terminology accurately.

### Interpreting at negotiations
The interpreter conveys not only the content but also the tone of the conversation, helping the parties reach agreement. We work both on site and online: the interpreter joins calls in Zoom, Teams or Google Meet.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between simultaneous and consecutive interpreting?
Consecutive interpreting follows each segment of the speech, so the event takes longer. Simultaneous interpreting runs at the same time as the speech but requires a booth, headphones and two interpreters.

### How far in advance should I book an interpreter for a conference?
It is best to book an interpreter in advance, and for simultaneous interpreting as early as possible, because equipment and two interpreters are needed. Urgent interpreting is also possible if an interpreter is available.
