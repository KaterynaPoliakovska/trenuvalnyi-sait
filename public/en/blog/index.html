Sworn translation

Sworn translation (tłumaczenie przysięgłe) is official certification of a document by a certified translator entered in the official register of the Ministry of Justice. Such a translation has full legal force in state institutions in Poland and EU countries.

How is sworn translation different from notarised translation?

The main difference is legal responsibility. A notary only certifies the authenticity of the translator's signature without checking the quality and accuracy of the text. A sworn translator, on the other hand, is personally responsible for every term and puts their own seal and signature, which makes the document valid in courts, voivodeship offices (Urząd) and educational institutions without additional procedures.

When is sworn translation required?

Sworn translation is required when submitting documents for a residence card (Karta Pobytu), applying to Polish universities, getting married abroad, registering a car or buying property.

Apostille and legalisation

For a document issued in one country to be recognised by official bodies of another state, it must go through international certification — an «Apostille» stamp or consular legalisation.

What is an «Apostille» stamp and where is it placed?

An apostille is a standardised international stamp that confirms the validity of an official's signature and seal on a document. It is placed by the relevant ministries (Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Ministry of Justice, Ministry of Education) of the country that issued the document.

The difference between an apostille and consular legalisation

An apostille is used for countries party to the Hague Convention. If the country where you are submitting documents has not joined the convention, a more complex two-step procedure applies — consular legalisation.

Useful tips on document translation

For document preparation to go quickly and without delays, it is worth keeping a few basic rules in mind before you contact a translation bureau.

How to avoid mistakes in the transliteration of names

Always give the manager or translator a copy of the first page of your international passport. This guarantees that the spelling of your name and surname in the translation will match your official documents 100%.

Requirements for the original document before translation

The document must be legible, not laminated, with clearly visible seals and signatures. Any corrections or damage may cause state bodies to refuse it.
